Attention mechanisms have fundamentally reshaped natural language processing, evolving from auxiliary components to foundational architectural principles. Initially introduced to augment recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and long short-term memory (LSTM) models, attention served as a mechanism to selectively focus on relevant parts of input sequences during decoding, mitigating the bottleneck of fixed-length context vectors and improving performance on tasks like machine translation [Attention]. However, these early architectures remained constrained by sequential computation, limiting parallelization and scalability.

The transformer architecture marked a paradigmatic rupture by dispensing with recurrence and convolutions entirely, relying instead solely on self-attention mechanisms to compute global dependencies between all positions in the input and output sequences [Attention]. This shift enabled unprecedented parallelization, faster training, and new state-of-the-art results across multiple benchmarks, establishing attention not as a supplementary feature but as the complete computational substrate of the model.

This survey critically examines the conceptual shift encapsulated by the phrase 'Attention → Attention': the transition from attention as an *additive mechanism* within sequential frameworks to attention as the *constitutive principle* of the entire architecture. We analyze how this evolution redefined representational capacity, computational efficiency, and the very ontology of sequence modeling, using the seminal 'Attention Is All You Need' paper as the pivotal exemplar of this transformation.
