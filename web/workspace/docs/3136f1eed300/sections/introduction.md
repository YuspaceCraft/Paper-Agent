The rapid evolution of artificial intelligence—particularly in transformer architectures, multimodal large language models (MLLMs), and domain-specific applications such as remote sensing and clinical natural language processing—has generated both transformative opportunities and persistent methodological challenges. These advances are critical for addressing real-world problems including environmental monitoring, disaster response, and clinical decision support; yet fragmentation across subfields, inconsistent evaluation practices, and limited cross-domain generalization hinder cumulative scientific progress. This review therefore aims to synthesize recent advances (2020–2024) in foundational AI techniques and their targeted applications, with three core objectives: (1) to map architectural innovations that improve efficiency, interpretability, and robustness in transformer-based models; (2) to analyze how multimodal fusion strategies adapt to domain-specific data modalities and constraints; and (3) to identify recurring gaps in evaluation rigor, benchmark standardization, and deployment readiness. Guided by these objectives, we address four research questions: (RQ1) How do recent diffusion-based and attention-refined models reconfigure representational learning in remote sensing change captioning? (RQ2) What design principles enable effective grounding of MLLMs in clinical or geospatial contexts? (RQ3) To what extent do current methods generalize across heterogeneous temporal, spatial, or semantic domains? (RQ4) What evaluation protocols best capture real-world utility beyond standard accuracy metrics? The review is structured as follows: Section 2 surveys related work across architectural foundations and application domains; Section 3 comparatively analyzes key methodological trade-offs using standardized criteria; Section 4 synthesizes cross-cutting themes, limitations, and emerging consensus; and Section 5 concludes with actionable recommendations for researchers and practitioners. Scope is explicitly bounded to peer-reviewed publications indexed in arXiv, IEEE Xplore, and ACM Digital Library; preprints without subsequent peer review, non-English works, and studies lacking reproducible methodology or empirical validation are excluded.
